#01 p01 · SITUATION REPORT · Islamabad, Mar 25 (24.1s)
  Video: drone footage of islamabad city pakistan (Pexels · Hamza Awan)
  Center caption: A {15-point} / ceasefire plan / handed to Tehran
  Narration: On March 25, Pakistan delivered a fifteen-point U.S. ceasefire plan to Iran. According to Pakistani officials, it offered sanctions relief in exchange for rolling back Iran's nuclear program, limits on its missiles, and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. The White House declined to discuss the plan, or to say whom in Iran it was negotiating with.

#02 p02 · SITUATION REPORT · Tehran, Mar 25 (22.7s)
  Video: traffic in the streets of tehran on a snowy winter day (Pexels · Abbas Malek Hosseini)
  Center caption: Iran's answer: / {No} / and {five conditions}
  Narration: That afternoon, Iranian state media, citing an unnamed senior official, said Iran rejected the American proposal. It laid out five conditions of its own: an end to attacks and to the killing of its leaders, guarantees that the war would not happen again, reparations, an end to fighting on every front, and Iranian sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz.

#03 p03 · SITUATION REPORT · Kuwait, Mar 25 (23.3s)
  Video: airplane takeoff at busy airport runway (Pexels · Rafael Minguet Delgado)
  Center caption: Kuwait airport's / {fuel depot} / burned for {three days}
  Narration: Earlier that day, an Iranian drone hit the fuel depot at Kuwait International Airport, starting a large fire. Kuwait said it shot down six other drones. The fire burned for three days and was not put out until March 28. Debris from an intercepted missile fell near Amman, and another missile landed in a field near Hadera in Israel.

#04 p04 · SITUATION REPORT · Tehran · NW Iran, Mar 25 (24.0s)
  Video: USS Abraham Lincoln supports Operation Epic Fury (998148).webm (Wikimedia Commons · U.S. Navy (DVIDS))
  Center caption: Papers moved, / but the {bombs} / did not stop
  Narration: In Tehran, two cruise missile production sites were destroyed. In northwestern Iran, airstrikes killed eleven people. Iran claimed it fired cruise missiles at the carrier USS Abraham Lincoln; the ship was not damaged. In western Anbar, a U.S. airstrike killed seven Iraqi soldiers. The papers were moving, and so were the bombs.

#05 p05 · SITUATION REPORT · Two Papers (21.2s)
  Video: a person holding a business summary (Pexels · Artem Podrez)
  Center caption: {15} and {5} / One shared word: / {Hormuz}
  Narration: One side said: give up what you have. The other said: pay for what you have done. Fifteen American points, five Iranian conditions. Only one word appeared on both papers. Hormuz. And the two countries meant opposite things by it. One paper went out, and one paper came back.

#06 p06 · Pakistan · Foreign Ministry, Islamabad — A Sheaf of Paper (25.5s)
  Video: a person holding a pencil (Pexels · kaboompics.com)
  Center caption: Paper does not / {evaporate}
  Narration: A sheaf of paper. Fifteen points. Tariq Mehmood, Pakistan's assistant secretary for West Asia, counted the pages first. Seven pages of hard English. Three weeks earlier he had memorized a number that belonged to no one. Seventy-two. It evaporated before it reached Washington. This time there was paper. Paper does not evaporate.

#07 p07 · Pakistan · Foreign Ministry, Islamabad (23.5s)
  Video: scenic aerial view of misty countryside farm (Pexels · Tom Fisk)
  Center caption: This city has carried / {other people's secrets}
  Narration: Outside, the Margalla Hills sat in morning haze. Islamabad was a city planned too well, a city that did not suit war. In the summer of 1971, Kissinger faked a stomach ache near here, vanished, and flew to Beijing. Even then, this city carried other people's secrets. Now it stood at the center of the world's most tangled war.

#08 p08 · Pakistan · Foreign Ministry, Islamabad (23.6s)
  Video: a person writing on notebook (Pexels · Artem Podrez)
  Center caption: Measuring the {distance} / between two papers
  Narration: The document was wrapped in diplomatic language. Unwrapped, its sentences were short. Put it down, and we will let you go. Tehran would not accept this paper. But a paper would come back. Measuring the distance between two papers was his job. The handover ended at ten. In his notebook he wrote only the time, not the contents.

#09 p09 · Langley, Virginia · CIA Iran Operations Center — The Third Resignation (24.9s)
  Video: a person using laptop while working (Pexels · kaboompics.com)
  Center caption: Three resignation letters. / {None saved}
  Narration: Leila Shirazi had written her resignation three times. The first, the day South Pars burned. The second, the dawn after Natanz was hit. The third, now, just before two in the morning. Each time she wrote it and did not save it. The cursor hovered over the button, she paused, and she closed the window. Tomorrow, then. Tomorrow was always another dawn.

#10 p10 · Langley, Virginia · CIA Iran Operations Center (25.6s)
  Video: woman using laptop (Pexels · ArtHouse Studio)
  Center caption: Numbers her hand wrote / went to {her aunt's city}
  Narration: The fifteen points had crossed her desk too. The technical annex on the nuclear clauses: centrifuge counts, enrichment caps, inspection cycles. She had written those numbers. Sentences from her own hand were now traveling through Islamabad to Tehran, the city where her aunt lived. The document went in the name of a nation. The numbers came from one person's hand.

#11 p11 · Langley, Virginia · CIA Iran Operations Center (25.3s)
  Video: drone video of a snow capped mountain range (Pexels · Alex Kad)
  Center caption: {Fordow}. / She typed it in herself
  Narration: The list of sites for inspection: Natanz, Isfahan, Arak. And Fordow. The draft from above had left Fordow out. The reason was one line: already inoperable. She typed the word in herself. Then she wrote her cable. If a counterproposal comes with the rejection, the door is not closed. Do not kill the counterproposal.

#12 p12 · Kuwait City · A Pharmacy in Salmiya — Clouds Are White (25.3s)
  Video: dramatic cityscape with rising smoke cloud (Pexels · Mytho Digital)
  Center caption: Fire was more frightening / in {daylight}
  Narration: Fire was more frightening by day. At night, fire is light. By day, it is smoke. Fatima al-Rashid, a pharmacist, saw it from behind her counter. A black column rose in the southern sky, over the airport. Her customers turned toward the glass door together. No one screamed. Twenty-six days of war had taken the screaming out of people.

#13 p13 · Kuwait City · A Pharmacy in Salmiya (24.4s)
  Video: woman arranging bottles of medicines on a rack (Pexels · cottonbro studio)
  Center caption: She did not / {charge} for the masks
  Narration: Burning jet fuel made a different smoke, and as a pharmacist she knew what was in it. In 1991, when a retreating army set the oil wells on fire, this country had lived once under a black sky. She took boxes of masks from the shelf and stacked them on the counter. She did not charge for them. Three people already wanted inhalers. She had eleven.

#14 p14 · Kuwait City · A Pharmacy in Salmiya (22.9s)
  Video: dynamic timelapse of fluffy white clouds (Pexels · Larkkid Dung)
  Center caption: "Mom, is that a {cloud}?" / "No. Clouds are white."
  Narration: The radio said Pakistan had carried the American paper to Tehran. One way, a paper crossed. The other way, drones came. Her five-year-old peeked out from under the counter. "Mom, is that a cloud?" "No. Clouds are white." Talks and bombs do not take turns. They come together, on the same day, under the same sky.

#15 p15 · Bahrain · Mina Salman, U.S. Fifth Fleet Liaison — A Price Tag (25.6s)
  Video: spirit of tasmania docked at hobart port (Pexels · Joolsmagools  ®️)
  Center caption: {15}: what America wants / {5}: what Iran demands
  Narration: Captain Kim Junho laid the two papers side by side. One, a summary of the fifteen points. The other, the five conditions just read on Iranian state television. Fifteen was what America wanted. Five was what Iran demanded. The distance between them was why the ships he knew by name were still at anchor. At Panmunjom, closing such a distance took two years.

#16 p16 · Bahrain · Mina Salman, U.S. Fifth Fleet Liaison (25.1s)
  Video: female hands grabbing cups of coffee (Pexels · cottonbro studio)
  Center caption: The first answer / has to be {"no"}
  Narration: Sarah Callahan came in with her notebook. "Iran said no. So is it over, Major?" Major Henderson set down his coffee. "Ever seen a negotiation where the first answer was yes? The first answer has to be no, so the next sentence can start. Rejection is part of the negotiation." Sarah wrote it down, then looked at Kim. "And you, Captain?"

#17 p17 · Bahrain · Mina Salman, U.S. Fifth Fleet Liaison (23.2s)
  Video: cargo container ships in port (Pexels · Tom Fisk)
  Center caption: "This is not a rejection. / It's a {price tag}."
  Narration: Kim pointed to the last line: sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz. "This is not a rejection. It's a price tag. Iran has already been choosing which ships may pass. For the ships I count, it doesn't matter who holds sovereignty. The door just has to open." He smiled. It was the first time in this war Sarah had seen him smile.

#18 p18 · Alborz Mountains, Iran · Air Defense Command Post — He Did Not Nod (23.0s)
  Video: aerial view of snow covered mountain range (Pexels · Ne Hayaldesin)
  Center caption: Scattered anger / became {five lines}
  Narration: Colonel Darius Sepahvand could guess who had written the five conditions. No single person. They were the words that had crossed the command network for three weeks. We must be paid. This must never happen again. The strait is ours. Scattered anger had gathered into five lines. And once words become writing, the writing becomes history.

#19 p19 · Alborz Mountains, Iran · Air Defense Command Post (25.0s)
  Video: winter sunset over snowy mountain landscape (Pexels · Alex Moliski)
  Center caption: "Father, what did the people / of {Kuwait} do to us?"
  Narration: The signals officer read out good news. Kuwait airport, fuel depot hit. A few men in the command post nodded. Darius should have nodded too. It was a professional reflex. The reflex rose to the back of his neck, and stopped. His daughter Narges's voice was there. Father, what did the people of Kuwait ever do to us? He had not answered her.

#20 p20 · Alborz Mountains, Iran · Air Defense Command Post (22.6s)
  Video: drone footage of a car in a desert (Pexels · K)
  Center caption: Today he / {did not nod}
  Narration: In the summer of 1988, the day it accepted a ceasefire, this country called it a poisoned chalice. If these conditions were met and the war ended, how would Narges see him? As a father who won? Or as the man who nodded when Kuwait's airport burned? Today he did not nod. No one noticed. It was the only thing he could do.

#21 p21 · Tehran · Classroom in the Hospital Basement — Cocoa (23.0s)
  Video: young girls drinking chocolate drink (Pexels · Kampus Production)
  Center caption: The children called him / {Grandpa}
  Narration: Seven-year-old Nariman came carrying a paper cup in both hands. Professor Azad Moradi had been in the hospital basement for six days. He had come for his blood pressure pills and found the children of doctors and nurses in the shelter. Schools had been closed for twenty-six days. He told them stories from the Shahnameh. The children called him Grandpa.

#22 p22 · Tehran · Classroom in the Hospital Basement (23.3s)
  Video: close up video of a hot drink (Pexels · cottonbro studio)
  Center caption: Even now, / {sweet things are sweet}
  Narration: "Grandpa, drink this if you're cold." The cocoa was warm. It was sweet. Even in war, cocoa was sweet. The world could change, and sweet things stayed sweet; that was what it meant for a civilization to go on. "Today some men from Pakistan brought a paper. But our country said no. Then why did they bring it, if we were going to say no?"

#23 p23 · Tehran · Classroom in the Hospital Basement (25.1s)
  Video: beautiful spring cherry blossoms on blue sky (Pexels · Ahmed ؜)
  Center caption: "After the war, / will {spring be prettier}?"
  Narration: "It's how grown-ups do it. First you say no once, and then the real talking starts." "That's weird." "Yes. It's weird." After a long while she asked, "Grandpa, when the war is over, will spring be prettier?" Again he could not answer. Far away a siren rose and fell silent. The two of them drank their cocoa without a word.

#24 p24 · Langley, Virginia · CIA Iran Operations Center, Night — Five Lines (27.5s)
  Video: intricate arabic calligraphy on stone monument (Pexels · Ahmed Terzioğlu)
  Center caption: Behind five sentences / stood {old wounds}
  Narration: Five lines arrived. Leila read them in the original Persian. An end to attacks and assassinations. Guarantees against another war. Reparations. An end to war on every front. Sovereignty over the strait. Behind each sentence stood an old wound: the coup of 1953, eight years of war, the years of sanctions. Not an enemy's history. Her aunt's country's history.

#25 p25 · Langley, Virginia · CIA Iran Operations Center, Night (22.8s)
  Video: woman typing on the laptop (Pexels · Monstera Production)
  Center caption: She saved it. / Then {deleted it}
  Narration: She opened the resignation file. This time she pressed save. Then she deleted it at once. Not tonight. The five lines had to be analyzed. She had to find a way to keep the Islamabad channel alive. If she left, no one in this building would be left to read Fordow's pulse. Midnight passed. She began to type.

#26 p26 · Langley, Virginia · CIA Iran Operations Center, Night (26.4s)
  Video: stunning sunrise over california beach (Pexels · Logan Voss)
  Center caption: In five lines, / no word of {nuclear}
  Narration: Ending assassinations and guarantees against recurrence: real demands. Reparations: leverage. Sovereignty over the strait: the wording cannot bend, but in practice it can be worked out through transit rules. And what was missing: nuclear. Not one word of enrichment. The documents that end wars usually come from the hands of those who wrote the documents that started them.
